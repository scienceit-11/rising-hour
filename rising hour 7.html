How this estimate is actually computed

Rectification is the traditional astrological practice of inferring an unrecorded birth time by testing candidate charts against known life events. It is not a scientifically validated method — no controlled study has shown these techniques predict outcomes beyond chance — so treat what follows as a documented hypothesis to test further, not a certificate.

Progressed Moon through the angles

The Moon appears to travel roughly one degree per day; secondary progression re-reads that as one day per year of life. This tool finds the Moon's progressed position for the birth-year-adjusted date of each event and measures its distance to the four chart angles (Ascendant, Descendant, Midheaven, Imum Coeli) for each candidate birth time — closer contact at more of your events favours that candidate.

Solar arc directions to the angles

The Sun's own progressed movement (about a degree per year) is applied to the natal angles directly, then checked for contact with the natal Sun and Moon at each event date — a second, independent classical technique for the same purpose.

The Part of Fortune, directed

The Part of Fortune (an "Arabic part" computed from the Ascendant, Sun and Moon, using the day-birth or night-birth formula as appropriate) moves at essentially the same rate as the Ascendant itself — about a degree every four minutes — which makes it just as time-sensitive, and a useful third, independent check. This tool directs it by the same solar arc and checks for contact with the natal angles, and separately checks the progressed Moon against the natal Part of Fortune, at each event date.

Physical appearance by rising sign

If you ticked any appearance traits, a fourth, separate tradition comes into play: the belief that the rising sign shows in physical features. The tool compares your selections against a keyword profile compiled from several published sources on rising-sign appearance and folds the match — averaged in equally alongside the three techniques above — into the ranking. Because the rising sign only changes roughly every two hours (faster or slower depending on latitude and time of year), this mainly narrows down which broad window of the day is plausible; the other techniques then do the finer work of pinpointing a time inside it. This is the least rigorous of the four signals — appearance-by-sign is folklore with no evidence behind it, sources disagree with each other, and any single person can look nothing like their sign's "typical" description — so a low or missing trait match on an otherwise strong candidate is not a reason to discard it.

The natal chart wheel

The wheel is drawn only for the single best-fit candidate above — not the full scanned window — since a chart needs one specific moment. Houses use the Placidus system (the most common Western default, dividing each quadrant's daily arc into thirds; verified during development to place the Ascendant and Midheaven exactly on cusps 1 and 10, keep all twelve cusps in strictly increasing order around the circle, and hold opposite cusps exactly 180° apart, checked from the equator to high latitudes). The other eight bodies use Astronomy Engine (see below) for geocentric apparent positions, light-time and aberration corrected. Aspect lines use conventional orbs (5–8° depending on the aspect) and are only ever a description of angles — no outcome is implied.

What's real astronomy here, and what isn't

Sidereal time, the Ascendant, Midheaven, and the Sun, Moon, and planet positions are computed using Astronomy Engine, an open-source (MIT-licensed) library built on the same class of JPL/VSOP87/ELP2000-based theory professional astronomy software uses. The astrological premise that these positions correlate with life events is a belief system, not an established finding — the math is exact, the interpretation is traditional.
